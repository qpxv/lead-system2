# 🧠 Client Acquisition SOP — Web Design on X

> **The meta-goal:** Never feel like a salesman. Every step should feel like you're just a cool guy who happens to build sick websites and wants to help people out.

---

## The Full Picture Before We Dive In

You're not pitching a service. You're having a conversation with someone, genuinely finding out what's holding them back, letting them figure it out themselves, and then casually dropping that you could literally fix that for free upfront. No pressure, no invoice, just "want me to build you something and you can decide after?"

The offer structure:
- You build the site/landing page first
- They see it, they love it, they pay (€800–€1,200)
- They don't love it → you keep a banger portfolio piece and move on

---

## Step 1 — The Opener

**Goal:** Get a reply. That's it. Nothing else matters here.

The opener works because it doesn't feel like outreach. It feels like a random person who actually watched their content saying something genuine. And on X especially, people are starved for real interactions because 90% of DMs are spam.

**The formula:** Genuine compliment → position it as private/personal → no ask whatsoever.

### ✅ Real Example Messages

> "Yoooooo I followed your content the past few days and you really know your stuff brother. Keep going 💪 (just wanted to say this 1 on 1 btw)"

> "bro your thread on [topic] was actually crazy good, don't see people breaking it down like that often. had to say something lol"

> "ngl I've been in this space a while and most content is the same recycled stuff but yours actually hits different. respect man 💪"

### ❌ What NOT to do

- Don't mention websites, design, business or anything remotely salesy
- Don't ask a question yet — just vibe
- Don't over-compliment to the point it reads fake ("your content is INCREDIBLE and LIFE CHANGING")
- Don't send a voice note or anything that requires effort from them to open

**What to expect:** 60%+ reply rate if you've already engaged with their content before sending. If you're cold cold with zero prior engagement, expect lower — warm them up first (like, comment genuinely for a few days before DMing).

---

## Step 2 — Build Rapport

**Goal:** Become a real human to them, not a vendor.

This is the most important step and also the one most people skip because they're impatient. Don't be that guy. The rapport is what makes the eventual "offer" land as friendly instead of salesy.

Talk about whatever comes up naturally. The goal is to feel like two people who just happened to meet online and clicked. Languages, their country, their journey, a shared interest — anything works. Be funny, be yourself, don't try to steer anything yet.

### ✅ Real Example Flow

> Them: "haha thanks man means a lot, been grinding for a while"

> You: "yeah it shows fr. how long have you been posting? feels like you've got the content thing figured out"

> Them: "like 8 months, it's been a journey lol"

> You: "8 months and already at this level, that's actually wild. where are you based btw?"

> Them: "I'm in Germany actually"

> You: "no way, I'm from Köln lol small world. your English is too clean for someone from Germany 😭"

Just chat. Don't force it. Two or three back-and-forths of genuine conversation is enough before you move to step 3.

---

## Step 3 — Transition Into Their Business

**Goal:** Move from small talk to understanding what they actually do, naturally.

The easiest transitions are curiosity-based. You're not interviewing them, you're just interested.

### ✅ Real Example Transitions

If it's obvious what they do (e.g. they're clearly a fitness coach):
> "how long have you been coaching? like is this your full time thing now or still building toward that?"

If it's less obvious:
> "so what's the actual business behind the content? like what do you sell / offer people?"

> "are you monetising the audience yet or still in that building phase?"

These questions feel natural because anyone who's been chatting with someone for a few messages would be curious about this stuff. It doesn't feel like a qualifying call — it feels like normal conversation.

---

## Step 4 — The Qualifying Questions

**Goal:** Understand their numbers so you know exactly where the gap is.

Ask these in a casual, conversational way — not as a list, not all at once. Let each answer breathe before you ask the next one.

### The Three Questions

**Q1 — Current reality:**
> "so how many clients are you working with right now?"

**Q2 — Past performance + monthly average:**
> "and like on average per month, what does that look like? like are some months way better than others or pretty consistent?"

**Q3 — Their goal:**
> "okay and where do you wanna be in like 90 days? like what's the number that would make you feel like things are properly moving?"

### ✅ Real Example Flow

> You: "so how many clients are you working with right now?"

> Them: "like 3-4 active at the moment"

> You: "okay nice, and how many are you pulling in on average a month? like roughly"

> Them: "honestly it varies a lot, some months 2, some months like 5 or 6"

> You: "yeah that inconsistency is the worst lol. so where do you wanna be in 90 days ideally? like if things were going well, what's the number?"

> Them: "man ideally like 8-10 a month consistently would change everything"

Now you know: they're doing 2-6/month inconsistently, want 8-10. That gap is your opening. Keep it in your head.

---

## Step 5 — Find the Bottleneck

**Goal:** Get them to identify what's stopping them from hitting their goal. NOT you telling them — them figuring it out.

This is the most important step because people believe what they say themselves way more than what someone else tells them. If they tell you "I think my online presence holds me back," that's 10x more powerful than you saying "your online presence is holding you back."

### ✅ Real Example

> You: "okay so 3-4 now, want 8-10 — what do you think is the main thing holding that back? like if you had to guess"

> Them: "honestly I think people check me out and then aren't sure if I'm legit enough to invest in"

> You: "interesting. what do you think makes someone look legit in this space? like what would make YOU trust someone enough to pay them?"

> Them: "probably like a clean website, good testimonials, a solid social presence"

**They just said it themselves.** Website. You didn't plant it. You just asked what legitimacy looks like.

### If They Don't Land on Website Naturally

Don't force it. Just ask one more casual question:

> "do you have a proper site right now or just the social stuff?"

> "what does someone see when they Google you or click your bio link?"

These naturally surface whether their web presence is actually solid or not — and 9 times out of 10, it isn't.

---

## Step 6 — The Socratic Back-and-Forth

**Goal:** Deepen their own realisation so they feel the gap themselves before you say anything.

This step is about not jumping in too fast. Once they've identified the problem, most people's instinct is to immediately pitch. Resist that. Ask one or two more questions that make them feel the weight of it themselves.

### ✅ Real Example

> Them: "I think people check me out and don't trust me enough"

> You: "what does your setup look like right now, like what do you have?"

> Them: "just an Instagram and a Linktree basically"

> You: "and you think someone who could pay like €2-3k for coaching is going to trust a Linktree?"

> Them: "lmaooo when you put it like that... probably not"

> You: "yeah exactly 😂 like there's a mismatch between how good your content is and what they land on when they go to actually hire you"

> Them: "that's actually so true bro"

Now they're sold on the problem. You haven't mentioned your service once.

---

## Step 7 — The Casual Mention

**Goal:** Drop the offer in a way that sounds like a friend going "oh wait, I could actually help with this."

This is not a pitch. There's no price. There's no pressure. It's almost throwaway — like you just remembered you could do something useful.

### ✅ Real Example Messages

> "yo wait actually — I build websites, that's literally what I do lol. I could just make you something and you can see if you even vibe with it before we talk money or anything"

> "actually bro this is exactly what I do 😂 I build landing pages and sites for people like you. I could literally just put something together for you to look at, no commitment or anything, just so you can see what it could look like"

> "ngl this is kind of funny timing — I build sites for coaches and creators, have been doing it for a while. I could throw something together for you just to see, worst case you don't like it and nothing changes lol"

### What Makes This Land

- You framed it as casual ("wait actually", "lol", "kind of funny")
- There's zero risk for them ("before we talk money", "no commitment")
- It's positioned as "just to see" — not "sign here"
- The offer removes every objection before they can raise it

---

## Step 8 — Handling Their "Yes"

When they say yes (and they will, because what's the downside for them), don't immediately go into logistics mode. Keep it chill.

> "sick, okay give me a few days and I'll send you something to look at. what's the main thing you want people to do when they hit the site — like book a call? buy something?"

Get the one key conversion goal. That's all you need to start. Don't bombard them with a brief yet.

---

## The Portfolio Angle (Remember This)

Every site you build — whether they pay or not — is a portfolio piece. One site a week = 50 sites in a year. Most agencies haven't built 50 sites ever. The "failed" deals aren't losses, they're free assets. Every no is a case study. That reframe alone makes this model almost impossible to lose at.

| Step | What You're Doing | What You're NOT Doing |
|------|-------------------|----------------------|
| 1. Opener | Giving a genuine compliment | Pitching anything |
| 2. Rapport | Being a normal human | Steering toward business |
| 3. Transition | Getting curious about their work | Asking about their website |
| 4. Qualifying | Understanding their numbers | Telling them what they need |
| 5. Bottleneck | Asking where they're stuck | Diagnosing for them |
| 6. Socratic loop | Deepening their own realisation | Jumping in with a solution |
| 7. Casual mention | Dropping the offer like a friend | Pitching like a salesperson |
| 8. Logistics | Getting the one key info you need | Sending a 10-question brief |
