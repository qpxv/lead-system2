# 💰 The Retainer Strategy — Turning One-Time Clients Into Monthly Income

---

## The Core Idea

The website isn't the product. The website is what gets them in the door. The retainer is the actual business.

€1,000 one-time vs €1,000 + €150/month for 12 months = €2,800 from one person. Same client, completely different business.

---

## The Handoff (Keep It Simple)

Never give them GitHub access. Never explain Vercel. They don't need to know any of it.

The setup is:
- They buy their domain (Namecheap, whatever — you walk them through it in 2 minutes)
- You point it at your Vercel project
- Done. From their side they just... have a website

They never touch anything technical. Which is exactly the setup that makes the retainer a no-brainer.

---

## When to Pitch the Retainer

Not at the start. Not during the build. At handoff — when the site is live, they've seen it, and they're hyped. That's peak excitement and peak leverage.

> "by the way, since everything runs on my setup I handle all the updates, speed, uptime, all of that. I keep it running for €100-150 a month — most people just keep that going so they never have to think about it"

That's the whole pitch. No features list. No hard sell. Just a casual mention at the exact moment they're most bought in.

---

## Why It Works

- The site lives on your Vercel — it literally can't run without you, which isn't manipulation, it's just true
- They have zero interest in learning GitHub and don't want to
- €150/month feels tiny compared to the €1,000 they just spent
- You're not selling maintenance, you're selling "never think about this again"

---

## The Numbers

| Scenario | Year 1 Revenue Per Client |
|----------|--------------------------|
| One-time only | €1,000 |
| One-time + retainer (12 months) | €2,800 |
| One-time + retainer (ongoing) | €1,000 + €150/mo forever |

Close 5 clients on retainer and you've got €750/month baseline before you've even opened your DMs that day.

---

## One Thing to Remember

Don't pitch the retainer as an upsell. Pitch it as the natural continuation — "since it's on my setup anyway." It's not an extra thing they're buying, it's just how the arrangement works. That framing makes it feel obvious rather than pushy.
